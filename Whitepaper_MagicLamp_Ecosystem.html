MagicLamp Ecosystem

Whitepaper · English edition · 26 November 2026

MagicLamp is a family of services and applications built on the Cardano blockchain. Its economy rests on three tokens, each with a single job. LAMP, the base token, is what people hold; it gives a voice in decisions and, placed in a personal vault, steadily produces MAGIC, the credit token people consume to use services. RING, the anchor token, is received by those who contribute resources to build the ecosystem. This paper explains how the pieces fit together, who is responsible for what, how decisions are made, and which parts are not built yet.

1. Three tokens, three jobs

Most token projects ask a single token to do everything: pay for things, store value and carry votes. Those roles pull against each other. MagicLamp separates them. LAMP decides who takes part and who has a say. MAGIC decides who can use a service right now. RING recognizes who has contributed to building it.

LAMP, the base token. A base token is the asset the rest of the economy rests on: MAGIC is produced from it, and it is the token that can be held in any ordinary wallet. Like ADA, LAMP moves freely between ordinary Cardano wallets, with no limit on where it can go. Putting it in a vault does not lock it: its owner can take it out at any time. Only LAMP its owner chooses to commit for voting is held for a set period (section 8). It is useful in two ways: kept in a personal or organizational vault it produces MAGIC (section 3), and it is one of the ingredients of a person's voice in decisions (section 8). It is designed for use inside the ecosystem, not as an investment.

MAGIC, the credit token. A credit token is a right to use services, recorded on the blockchain and tied to one identity. MAGIC is locked in the vault of the Phoenix DID that owns it. It is closer to an allowance than to money. It cannot be handed to someone else, it slowly fades if nobody uses it, and it disappears once consumed. The project runs no market for MAGIC and supports none. MAGIC is not operating yet.

RING, the anchor token. An anchor token ties recognition to real contributions. It is received only for resources actually contributed to the ecosystem, and it is designed to move only between addresses that belong to Phoenix DIDs, so every unit stays with someone the ecosystem can recognize. It is not a general currency; the project provides no trading for it and promises nothing about its price. Its detailed rules are set out in the RING paper.

Two kinds of token

The word token is used in two senses, and the three tokens do not all fit the same one. In the broad sense, a token is a unit whose creation, use and limits are enforced by contracts on a blockchain rather than by a company's own records. All three are tokens in that sense, which is why they form one model: each is a different answer to the question of what a contract should let a unit do.

In the narrow sense used by Cardano wallets, a token is a native asset: a separate item, identified by its own minting policy, that sits in an address and can be sent from one address to another. LAMP and RING are native assets. MAGIC is not. It exists only as a balance written into the on-chain record of each vault and tied to the identity that owns the vault. The contract that keeps that record allows MAGIC to be produced, consumed, to fade or to expire, and nothing else. There is no asset that could leave the vault, so MAGIC cannot be sent, sold or held in a wallet. A wallet that shows an asset named MAGIC is showing something else.

This difference is deliberate. A unit that can move between addresses can be traded, and a unit that can be traded gets a price. Keeping MAGIC inside the vault keeps the right to use services separate from anything that could be bought and sold.

2. How the ecosystem is built

MagicLamp is organized much like a city. Three layers are built: roads and utilities at the bottom (infrastructure), public services in the middle (platforms), and the shops and homes people actually visit on top (applications). Each relies on the one below it and serves the one above. Above them stand the people and organizations the city exists for, the participants: users who consume MAGIC to use services, and contributors who supply resources and receive RING.

Infrastructure

Infrastructure is what every other part relies on and what no one uses for its own sake. It runs on the Cardano blockchain, which records every transfer publicly and cannot be quietly rewritten, and it has four parts.

  • Token contracts create LAMP within its ceiling, keep the MAGIC balance of every vault, and issue RING.
  • Phoenix Identity, provided by PhoenixKey, gives each person, company or service one verified digital identity (a DID) whose keys belong to its owner.
  • LampNet DePIN is a network of devices: people lend spare computing power, storage and bandwidth to it, receive RING for what is measured, and are also recognized through the Join LampNet share (section 5).
  • VeData Oracle collects data from the real world, classifies it and anchors it on Cardano, so that contracts and applications can rely on it.

Platforms

A platform is a shared service that applications are built on, rather than a product people open for its own sake. Each platform has its own DID and shares in LAMP according to how much the ecosystem actually uses it.

  • AladinWork: work and jobs.
  • OriLife: traceability, recording where a product comes from and what has happened to it.
  • ProofChat: communication.
  • AffiSo: affiliate and referral.
  • SuperApp: an app builder. A new app is a set of settings on one shared code base rather than a new program.
  • TigerAgent: AI agents designed to run on LampNet.

Applications

The application layer is where the ecosystem meets people, and its role is the user experience. An application turns the services of one or more platforms into screens, steps and plain language that a person can follow, and it keeps out of sight what people should not have to handle themselves, such as keys, network fees and contracts. Users consume MAGIC through applications, and contributors offer their resources through them. Aladin and CheckFarm are two applications, and anyone in the community can build more, as long as each relies on at least one platform. Applications share in LAMP according to real use. A team that offers both a platform and an application registers them separately, so the two are never counted twice.

Participants: users and contributors

Participants are not something the ecosystem builds; they are who it is built for. Each participant, whether a person or an organization, takes part through its own Phoenix DID, and the same participant can play both roles.

  • Users are the people and organizations that use the ecosystem's services. They consume MAGIC, which the LAMP in their own vault produces (section 3).
  • Contributors are the people and organizations that supply the resources the ecosystem needs in order to grow. They receive RING as recognition of what they contribute.

3. From LAMP to MAGIC

Every participant in MagicLamp, whether a person, a company or a service, is known by a DID, a digital identity whose keys belong to its owner rather than to a platform. Each DID can have a vault for LAMP.

LAMP placed in a vault produces a little MAGIC every epoch, Cardano's natural rhythm of about five days. Producing MAGIC does not use the LAMP up, and the owner can take it out of the vault at any time; from then on it simply stops producing MAGIC. The same LAMP sitting in an ordinary wallet produces nothing. The reason is simple: the MAGIC that services consume should come from participants who can be recognized, not from anonymous balances.

Two other kinds of container hold LAMP but produce no MAGIC. A store holds LAMP that has not been handed out yet. A safe holds LAMP for one person, or for one list of recipients, under its own rules. The single exception is the Wakeme safe (section 6): it belongs to one person's DID, so its LAMP produces MAGIC as in a vault, but unlike a vault it cannot be taken out until its 1001 days have passed.

4. A ceiling, not a stockpile

There will never be more than 36,000,000,000 LAMP. That number is a ceiling, not a pile created on day one. LAMP comes into existence only when it is due to be handed out, following a published schedule; until then it simply does not exist. A block explorer will therefore show far fewer than 36 billion LAMP for a long time. That is intended.

Four promises are written into the contracts themselves, not just into this paper:

  • The ceiling cannot be raised. Every token on Cardano is defined by a rule set called a policy. LAMP's policy keeps a running count that can only go up and refuses anything beyond 36 billion. Raising the ceiling would mean creating a different token.
  • Nothing is destroyed. LAMP is never burned. Whatever goes unclaimed returns to the Treasury, a shared account, where it is recorded rather than erased.
  • New LAMP is always held first. Freshly created LAMP lands in a store with its own rules, never directly in someone's wallet.
  • Every amount is exact. LAMP is counted in whole millionths, so no rounding errors can creep in.

Because official LAMP does not exist yet, these promises can be read in the code but not yet observed on the live network.

6. How people receive LAMP

LAMP is never sold for money: there is no ICO and no presale. Every share hands out LAMP according to a public formula or condition. The only exception, SRCL, is described below and is not open. What people do with their LAMP afterwards is their own affair.

A little at a time

Ten shares release LAMP gradually, epoch after epoch, never faster than a set limit. If a limit goes unused it carries over rather than being lost. A committee may adjust the pace, but only within bounds written into the contract, and any change is visible to everyone.

Wakeme

Wakeme gives each person one personal safe of up to 1001 LAMP for using services. It is not a loan: there is no debt, no interest and no lender. Over 1001 days, the safe lets its owner use one more LAMP each day inside the ecosystem, without handing that LAMP over. Only after the 1001 days, and only for someone whose identity has been verified, can LAMP leave the safe for a wallet of their choice, in step with how much MAGIC they have used. A safe that stays idle for a long time is closed and its LAMP goes back to the shared pool.

Airdrop

The Airdrop thanks people who help keep Cardano secure by delegating their ADA, along with the operators of stake pools. Its deeper purpose is to bring together a community willing to take part in governance. Participants register in advance and need more than 1,000 ADA. Once the list is fixed, each person claims their part with a short proof that they are on it, without the whole list ever being published. Claiming costs the recipient nothing.

ETD

ETD (Early TIGER Delegated) thanks the early supporters of the TIGER stake pool. It is planned to run first, as a real-world test of the whole system on Cardano's main network. The list was fixed by the last snapshot taken before midnight UTC on 18 June 2026. Once it runs, LAMP is released gradually, straight into each person's wallet.

SRCL, the one exception, not open

SRCL would let someone direct their ADA staking rewards (never their savings, which stay in their own wallet) toward paying other users' network fees, receiving LAMP in return by a public formula. Because those rewards have value, this is an exchange, and this paper says so plainly. SRCL stays closed until MagicLamp DAO exists and three seats are filled. No SRCL address has been published. Do not send ADA to anyone claiming to collect it for SRCL.

7. Who does what

Four bodies have a part in MagicLamp. MagicLamp Capital, now being established, is to issue the token, GreenSun Tech researches and designs it, Aladin Contract builds it, and MagicLamp Foundation, formed by the community, is to run the ecosystem in the future as a DAO.

MagicLamp Capital

MagicLamp Capital is a company being established outside Vietnam to be the initial issuer of LAMP. Once established, it launches official LAMP on the main network and publishes its policy ID (section 9). The key that does this will be controlled by MagicLamp Capital under a board resolution; who may sign will be set and changed only by resolution, and each change will be published. MagicLamp Capital is to hold share 9 (1.296 billion LAMP, 3.6%), released at the same slow pace as the community's, and is to commit never to sell that LAMP. Proof of its formation and of that commitment will be published at magiclamp.eco.

GreenSun Tech

GreenSun Tech is the research and design company. It works out the token model, the governance rules and the contract specifications. GreenSun Tech holds share 11, one sixth of the ceiling (6 billion LAMP), released at the same slow pace as everyone else.

Aladin Contract

Aladin Contract is the building company. It writes and tests the open-source code that runs MagicLamp and develops applications on it, including Aladin. It holds share 10, also 6 billion LAMP, released at the same slow pace.

Both GreenSun Tech and Aladin Contract are Vietnamese companies. Neither holds the key that launches the token, and neither signs the contracts that create, hand out, sell or exchange tokens on the main network.

MagicLamp Foundation

MagicLamp Foundation is the organization the community forms to run the ecosystem in the future. It looks after the people who use the ecosystem and helps their number grow, and it makes decisions as a DAO, MagicLamp DAO: the community voting under published rules, one verified person per voter (section 8). Once it is operating, it decides how the Treasury and the Development fund are used and adjusts the governance settings. Until then, committees act within limits written into the contracts, and every action they take is public.

The Foundation widens its circle step by step, bringing in partners and members of the community, and also the three companies, until it has at least 21 seats. The companies' place in it is limited on purpose:

  • At most 3 seats, one per company, however many seats there are. Under the voting rules in section 8 no voter counts for more than one twenty-first, so 3 voters can neither block nor carry a decision.
  • No veto, no chair and no permanent seat.
  • A time limit: these seats end after a fixed number of epochs and are then filled by election like any other.
  • The two Vietnamese companies take no part in votes on creating, selling or exchanging tokens or on spending from shared funds.

The Foundation is not a share and receives no share of LAMP of its own.

8. How decisions are made

In many token projects one token means one vote, so whoever owns the most decides. MagicLamp counts people instead. Every voter is an individual verified through Phoenix Identity, an identity system based on biometrics, so that nobody can multiply their voice by opening more wallets.

A person's weight in a vote comes from four ingredients, multiplied together, each with its own upper limit:

The formula

Write C1 to C4 for the four ingredients of one voter, cap for the upper limit of each and w for its weight. Each ingredient is first cut down to its limit, then raised to its weight, and the four results are multiplied:

VP = min(C1, cap1)w1 × min(C2, cap2)w2 × min(C3, cap3)w3 × min(C4, cap4)w4

Table 5. The four ingredients of voting power.

IngredientWhat is measuredLimit
C1 MAGIC usedMAGIC the person has actually consumed over roughly the last 18 epochsset by the DAO
C2 LAMP committedLAMP the person has chosen to commit for roughly the next 24 epochs; it cannot be withdrawn until the commitment endsset by the DAO
C3 Reputationstanding earned in the community; the measure is not published yetset by the DAO
C4 LAMP heldLAMP the person holds today100 million LAMP

Multiplying rather than adding is deliberate. With a sum, a wealthy voter could reach the top of the two LAMP ingredients and still score high with no reputation at all. With a product, a weak ingredient pulls the whole result down.

A second rule sits on top. No single voter may count for more than one twenty-first of all voting power, about 4.8%:

counted VP = min(VP, total VP ÷ 21)

So at least 8 independent voters are needed to hold more than a third of the vote, and at least 14 to reach two thirds. The number 21 is a starting floor, not a fixed council. As more people take part, the limit stops being reached and matters less.

Consuming MAGIC is not proof of genuine engagement on its own. Because MAGIC can be produced from LAMP, influence through C1 can be bought indirectly. The limit on C1 keeps that price high and finite, but it does not rule it out.

The DAO can adjust the limits and weights of these ingredients.

What the design does not solve yet

  • Spreading LAMP across people. Because LAMP moves freely, an organization could spread its LAMP across the vaults of its own members. To use 6 billion LAMP this way it would need about 60 different identities, each with its own MAGIC use, commitment and reputation. "One person, one identity" is an assumption that nothing on the blockchain enforces yet. This limit is stated openly; it is not closed.
  • MAGIC held by organizations. A large part of all LAMP will sit in the vaults of companies and platforms. Only MAGIC used by verified individuals is meant to count toward votes. Until the rule for organizations is settled and enforced, their MAGIC does not count.
  • Reputation. How reputation will be measured has not been published, so the voting formula is not in force yet.

9. Recognizing genuine LAMP

Anyone can create a token called LAMP. Only one 56-character code, the policy ID, identifies the real one.

Until the official policy ID is published, no token is genuine LAMP. After that, only the ID published on magiclamp.eco is. If the policy is ever replaced, the new ID will be announced in full.

11. Risks and limits

  • There may never be a market. There is no market for LAMP today. The project has no plan to list it and promises nothing about its price.
  • Contracts can have flaws. They have been tested and reviewed internally, but not audited independently. Once released, a policy cannot be changed; fixing a flaw would mean moving to a different token.
  • The ceiling belongs to one policy. Nothing in the code forces a future replacement to keep the same ceiling.
  • Committees have real powers for now. Until the DAO takes over, committees can grant LAMP and move allocations within fixed bounds. The four promises in section 4 are enforced by the contracts; the split into shares in section 5 is not, which is why it is public and every transfer can be checked.
  • You hold your own keys. Lose a wallet's key or recovery phrase and the LAMP in it is lost.
  • Beware of copies. Fake tokens and fake airdrops may appear. Trust only the full policy ID and official channels (section 9).
  • Law differs from place to place. How a token is classified depends on the law where you live, not on what its makers say. Check, or ask for advice.

12. Who LAMP is not for

LAMP is not intended for residents or citizens of Vietnam. It is also not intended for citizens or residents of the United States (US persons as defined in the US securities rule known as Regulation S). Nor is it intended for anyone on the sanctions lists of the United Nations, the United States (kept by its Office of Foreign Assets Control, OFAC), the European Union or the United Kingdom, or for anyone in a country or territory under comprehensive sanctions.

A check that keeps these groups out must be in place before LAMP is handed out on the main network. It has not been built yet. Today the only barrier is a block by internet address in the ETD service, which cannot tell where someone lives or what nationality they hold. Everyone who receives LAMP remains responsible for obeying the law where they are and the law of their nationality.
