1. What the MagicLamp ecosystem is

An example. A farmer records the harvest of a plot and submits the records with evidence. A service that rates the evidence and anchors it on a public blockchain makes the record checkable by others. The farmer is recognized for that contribution. Another person, who holds LAMP in a personal vault, uses a small amount of MAGIC every few days to access a service in an application. Both people take part through the same kind of digital identity.

The definition. MagicLamp is a family of services and applications built on Cardano, a public blockchain that records every transfer openly and cannot be quietly rewritten. Its economy rests on three tokens, each with one job. A token is a unit whose creation, use and limits are enforced by contracts on a blockchain, rather than by a company's own records.

  • LAMP is the base token. It is the asset the rest of the economy rests on.
  • MAGIC is the credit token. It is the right to use services.
  • RING is the anchor token. It recognizes people and organizations that contribute real resources.

The problem it addresses. Many token projects ask a single token to do everything: carry votes, store value and buy services. Those roles pull against each other. MagicLamp separates them. LAMP decides who takes part and who has a say. MAGIC decides who can use a service right now. RING recognizes who has contributed to building the ecosystem.

2. How it works

The ecosystem is organized like a city. Roads and utilities are at the bottom (infrastructure). Public services are in the middle (platforms). The shops and homes people actually visit are on top (applications). Above them stand the people and organizations the city exists for (participants).

Participants

Participants are not something the ecosystem builds. They are who it is built for. Each takes part through its own Phoenix DID. A DID is a decentralized identifier: a digital identity whose keys belong to its owner rather than to a platform. It can stand for a person, a company or a service. Phoenix DIDs are issued through Phoenix Identity, provided by PhoenixKey. The same participant can play both roles below.

  • Users are the people and organizations that use the ecosystem's services. They consume MAGIC.
  • Contributors are the people and organizations that supply the resources the ecosystem needs in order to grow. They are recognized with RING.

Applications

An application is where the ecosystem meets people. It turns the services of one or more platforms into screens and plain steps, and it keeps out of sight what people should not have to handle themselves, such as keys, network fees and contracts. Aladin and CheckFarm are two applications. Anyone in the community can build more, as long as each relies on at least one platform.

Platforms

A platform is a shared service that applications are built on. People do not open it for its own sake.

  • AladinWork: work and jobs.
  • OriLife: traceability, recording where a product comes from and what has happened to it.
  • ProofChat: communication.
  • AffiSo: affiliate and referral.
  • SuperApp: an app builder, where a new app is a set of settings on one shared code base.
  • TigerAgent: AI agents designed to run on LampNet.

Infrastructure

Infrastructure is what every other part relies on. It has four parts.

  • Token contracts are designed to create LAMP within its ceiling, keep the MAGIC balance of every vault, and issue RING.
  • Phoenix Identity gives each person, company or service one verified digital identity.
  • LampNet DePIN is a network of devices. DePIN means a network of physical devices owned by many people rather than one company. People lend spare computing power, storage and bandwidth to it.
  • VeData Oracle collects data from the real world, classifies it and anchors it on Cardano. An oracle is a service that brings real-world facts onto a blockchain. To anchor is to write a short fingerprint of a record on the blockchain, so anyone can later check that the record has not been changed.

The three tokens

LAMP, the base token. LAMP moves freely between ordinary Cardano wallets, like ADA, the currency of Cardano. It has two uses. Kept in a personal or organizational vault, it produces MAGIC. It is also one ingredient of a person's voice in decisions (section 7). It is designed for use inside the ecosystem, not as an investment.

MAGIC, the credit token. MAGIC is a right to use services, recorded on the blockchain and tied to one identity. It is closer to an allowance than to money. It cannot be handed to someone else. It slowly fades if nobody uses it, and it is consumed when a service uses it. The project runs no market for MAGIC and supports none. MAGIC is not operating yet.

RING, the anchor token. RING ties recognition to real contributions. It recognizes resources actually contributed to the ecosystem. It is designed to move only between Phoenix DID addresses, so every unit stays with someone the ecosystem can recognize. RING is recognition and is not payment for work. It is not a general currency. The project provides no trading for it and promises nothing about its price. Its detailed rules are to be published separately.

MAGIC differs from the other two in a technical way. LAMP and RING are Cardano native assets, which are separate items identified by their own policy that sit in an address and can be sent. MAGIC is only a balance written into the on-chain record of each vault. It cannot leave the vault, so it cannot be sent, sold or held in a wallet. A wallet that shows an asset named MAGIC is showing something else. This is deliberate: a unit that can move can be traded, and a unit that can be traded gets a price.

From LAMP to MAGIC

A vault is a place for LAMP that belongs to one DID. LAMP placed in a vault produces a little MAGIC every epoch, which is Cardano's cycle of about five days. Producing MAGIC does not use the LAMP up. The owner can take it out of the vault, except LAMP committed under a published condition, and from then on it stops producing MAGIC. The same LAMP in an ordinary wallet produces nothing. The reason is that the MAGIC services consume should come from participants who can be recognized, not from anonymous balances.

A ceiling, not a stockpile

There will never be more than 36,000,000,000 LAMP. This number is a ceiling. It is not a pile created on day one. LAMP is minted gradually, only when it is due to be handed out under a published schedule, and until then it does not exist. A block explorer, which is a public website that shows every transaction and token, will therefore show far fewer than 36 billion LAMP for a long time. That is intended.

Burning LAMP is not permitted. LAMP that goes unclaimed moves into the Treasury, a shared account where it is recorded. LAMP leaves circulation only by moving into the Treasury; it is never burned. The 36 billion is divided into 18 shares, each set aside for one purpose; the full table is in the Tokenomics paper.

Each of these rules is written into the contracts, not only into this paper. Once official LAMP is on the main network, anyone can check them there.

3. What value it brings

The paper describes what the design is meant to provide. It does not describe any financial benefit.

  • A role for each token. Having one job per token keeps the right to use a service separate from anything that can be bought and sold.
  • Identity-based participation. Because MAGIC comes from LAMP in vaults of recognized identities, services are used by participants who can be recognized.
  • Recognition of real contribution. Devices, work, knowledge, data, referrals, software and content can each be contributed through a named part of the ecosystem. The part measures or confirms the contribution. It never relies on the contributor's own word.
  • Decisions by people. Governance counts verified individuals rather than token weight (section 7).
  • Public records. Every transfer of LAMP can be checked on a public blockchain.
  • Use rather than speculation. LAMP is designed for use inside the ecosystem. The project does not position it as an investment product, does not publish or promise a price, makes no commitment to list it, and does not operate a secondary market.

4. Who it is for

The ecosystem is built for people and organizations who can offer a resource or use a service. Resources fall into six groups. Each is contributed through a particular part of the ecosystem, which measures or confirms that the contribution really happened.

  • A device owner. A person with a spare laptop or server joins LampNet DePIN. The network places the device in one of four tiers from its measured hardware and meters the storage and bandwidth it actually serves. A computer with enough memory can also run AI models for TigerAgent.
  • A worker. A person with a skill, such as programming, design, repairing an air conditioner, driving, tutoring or farm work, takes a job on AladinWork. The worker submits evidence, the person who requested the job confirms completion or opens a dispute, and each side rates the other.
  • An expert. A doctor, lawyer, engineer or teacher offers a consultation, a lesson or a review as a booked job on AladinWork.
  • A farmer. A farmer submits records of animals, crops or harvests with evidence, through VeData Oracle, OriLife or CheckFarm. VeData rates the quality of the evidence, classifies the record and anchors it on Cardano.
  • A community connector. A person with a network brings in new participants through a personal referral link tied to a Phoenix DID, on AffiSo. Recognition follows published conditions for each newcomer who qualifies.
  • A developer or creator. A developer builds applications on SuperApp or contributes code, which is accepted only once reviewed and merged. A creator prepares content and channel settings for the publishing agents of TigerAgent.
  • A user. A person or business that wants to use a service in an application holds LAMP in a vault and consumes the MAGIC it produces.

Three rules apply to every group. A contribution is tied to the contributor's Phoenix DID. It counts only once the part of the ecosystem it went through has measured or confirmed it. The amount of RING recognizing it follows the published RING rules. Some contributions are also recognized with LAMP, for example Join LampNet for devices.

Data means data the contributor has the right to share. RING is never given in exchange for personal data, and personal data is used only with the person's consent and for the stated purpose. RING is not payment for work under any employment or service contract, and it carries no promise of monetary value. Anyone who receives RING is responsible for their own tax position.

5. How people receive LAMP

The distribution design has no ICO and no presale. No pot releases LAMP in exchange for money or other assets, except SRCL, which is not yet activated (see below). Every share hands out LAMP under a public formula or a condition specific to that share. What a recipient does with LAMP afterwards is a separate matter.

New LAMP is first held in a store, which is a container for LAMP not handed out yet. From the store it reaches people by four kinds of route.

  • A little at a time. Ten shares release LAMP gradually, epoch after epoch, never faster than a set limit.
  • Wakeme. Each person can open one personal safe of up to 1001 LAMP for using services. It is not a loan. There is no debt, no interest and no lender. Use opens up day by day, and nothing reaches a wallet during the first 1001 days. A safe is a container for LAMP belonging to one person or one list of recipients, under its own rules.
  • Published list (Airdrop). It thanks people who help secure Cardano by delegating ADA, and operators of stake pools. Its deeper purpose is to bring together a community willing to take part in governance. A person registers in advance, the list is fixed by a snapshot (a record of who held or delegated what at one moment), and each person claims their part. Claiming costs the recipient nothing.
  • Scheduled release (ETD). It thanks early supporters of the TIGER stake pool. It is planned as the first real-world run of the system on the main network.

SRCL, the one exception, not open. SRCL would let a person direct their ADA staking rewards, never their savings, toward paying other users' network fees, and receive LAMP by a public formula. Because those rewards have value, this is an exchange. SRCL stays closed until MagicLamp DAO exists and three seats are filled. No SRCL address has been published. Do not send ADA to anyone claiming to collect it for SRCL.

For the shares, release schedules, routes and eligibility of each round, read the Distribution paper and the Tokenomics paper at https://magiclamp.eco/tokenomics. This paper does not repeat them.

6. Who does what

Four bodies have a part in MagicLamp. One company is being established to issue the token, two companies build and design the system, and the community is to form an organization to run it.

MagicLamp Capital is the issuer being established. The issuer of LAMP is a legal entity outside Vietnam that has not been established yet; it will be announced once established. It is planned as MagicLamp Capital Ltd. (BVI). Once formed, it is planned to commit to selling no LAMP, and it will hold one share of 1.296 billion LAMP (3.60%), released at the same slow pace as the community shares. That commitment is planned and not yet made, and it is not an on-chain rule. Proof of the company's formation and of the commitment will be published at https://magiclamp.eco.

GreenSun Tech is a research and design company. It works out the token model, the governance rules and the contract specifications. It holds a share of 6 billion LAMP.

Aladin Contract is the building company. It writes and tests the open-source code that runs MagicLamp and develops applications on it, including Aladin. It holds a share of 6 billion LAMP. Both companies are in Vietnam, and their legal structure is being finalized.

Who deploys and signs. On Mainnet, any script that mints, grants, sells or swaps a token of the system is deployed and signed by the DAO. The software developer only builds software to order. It does not deploy or sign those scripts on Mainnet.

MagicLamp Foundation is the organization the community is to form to run the ecosystem in the future. It is not an existing legal entity, and the project does not set it up. It is to decide as a DAO, MagicLamp DAO: the community voting under published rules, one verified person per voter. A DAO is a community that decides by vote under published rules. Once it operates, it is to decide how the Treasury and the Development fund are used and to adjust the governance settings. Until then, committees act within limits written into the contracts, and every action is public.

The Foundation is to have 21 seats, filled by people the community nominates. The three companies hold three of them, one each. Their place is limited on purpose: no veto, no chair, no permanent seat, and a time limit after which these seats are filled by election. The two Vietnamese companies take no part in votes on creating, selling or exchanging tokens or on spending from shared funds. The Foundation is not a share and receives no LAMP of its own.

Until control passes to the DAO, some powers stay with committees. They can grant entitlements and move an allocation to another script, within caps. The contracts do not enforce the table of shares itself. This is why the table is published and every transfer can be checked.

7. How decisions are made

In many token projects one token means one vote, so whoever owns the most decides. MagicLamp counts people instead.

An example. Take two people. One holds a great deal of LAMP and does nothing else. The other holds little but uses services, commits LAMP and has standing in the community. The larger holder does not receive many times the weight, because each ingredient of voting power has its own cap.

Every voter is one person with one verified Phoenix DID. This is a decentralized identity system based on biometrics, meant to stop one person from creating many wallets to gain many votes. Voting power combines several factors: MAGIC consumed, LAMP committed, reputation and LAMP held. They are multiplied together, and each has a cap, including a cap on the LAMP a voter holds. If one factor is zero, the weight is zero. The DAO can adjust most caps and weights once it exists.

Multiplying rather than adding is deliberate. With a sum, a wealthy voter could reach the top of the LAMP factors and still score high with no reputation at all. With a product, a weak factor pulls the whole result down. The exact formula and its parameters are set through governance and are not repeated here.

What the design does not solve yet.

  • Spreading LAMP across people. Because LAMP moves freely, an organization could spread its LAMP across the vaults of its own members. Doing this at scale would need many different identities, each with its own MAGIC use, commitment and reputation. One person, one identity is an assumption that nothing on the blockchain enforces yet. This limit is stated openly. It is not closed.
  • MAGIC held by organizations. A large part of all LAMP will sit in vaults of companies and platforms. Only MAGIC consumed by verified individuals is meant to count toward votes. Until the rule for organizations is settled and enforced, their MAGIC does not count.
  • Reputation. How reputation is measured will be published before it counts toward votes.
  • Consuming MAGIC is not proof of genuine engagement on its own. MAGIC can be produced from LAMP, so influence through it can be bought indirectly. Its cap keeps the cost of buying influence this way high and bounded, but does not rule it out.

The Governance paper, at https://magiclamp.eco/governance, describes the model in full: who takes part, how voting power is earned, how people are chosen for roles, how a decision is made and who has authority over what. Conditions and timing for handing over to the DAO follow the development roadmap.

8. Recognizing genuine LAMP

Anyone can create a token called LAMP. Only one 56-character code, the policy ID, identifies the real one. A policy is the rule set that defines a token on Cardano, and the policy ID is the code that identifies it.

  1. Open an independent Cardano lookup site, for example cexplorer.io. Use only links taken from this paper or from https://magiclamp.eco. Do not click links received in messages or advertisements.
  2. Paste the policy ID into the site's search box.
  3. Match all 56 hexadecimal characters, not just the first few. A fake token may share the first few characters and carry an identical display name.

Until the official policy ID is published, no token is genuine LAMP. After that, only the policy ID published in this paper and at https://magiclamp.eco is. If the policy is ever replaced, the new ID will be announced in full. A replaced policy means a different token.

10. Risks and limits

  • There may never be a market. There is no market for LAMP today. The project has no plan to list it and promises nothing about its price.
  • Contracts can have flaws. They have been tested and reviewed internally, but not audited independently. A released policy cannot be modified. A defect would have to be fixed by a different policy, which means a different token.
  • The ceiling belongs to one policy. Nothing in the code forces a future replacement policy to carry the same ceiling. The project commits to announcing every replacement with the full 56-character policy ID.
  • Committees have real powers for now. Until the DAO takes over, committees can grant LAMP and move allocations within fixed bounds.
  • Parts are unfinished. MAGIC, governance, the DAO and some shares are not yet operating or not yet decided.
  • You hold your own keys. A wallet is software that holds the keys to your tokens, and whoever holds the keys controls the tokens. If the key or recovery phrase is lost, the LAMP in that wallet is lost.
  • Beware of copies. Lookalike tokens and fake airdrops may appear. Trust only the full policy ID and official channels.
  • Law differs from place to place. How a crypto-asset is classified depends on the law of each jurisdiction, not on what its makers say. Each person should check, or seek legal advice, in their own jurisdiction. The project has no independent legal opinion, and it does not claim compliance with the law of any jurisdiction.

11. Who LAMP is not for

LAMP is not intended for residents or citizens of Vietnam.

LAMP is also not intended for citizens or residents of the United States (US persons under Regulation S), persons on the sanctions lists of the United Nations, the United States (OFAC), the EU or the United Kingdom, or persons in a country or territory under comprehensive sanctions.

A check that excludes these persons must be in place before LAMP is granted on Mainnet. That check is not in the code yet, and Mainnet has not launched. No on-chain constraint blocks receiving LAMP by jurisdiction or nationality. The only block today is off-chain and based on access location, in the ETD registration and withdrawal service. It cannot determine residence or nationality, so it does not replace the check. Each recipient remains responsible for complying with the law where they are and the law of their nationality.

12. Glossary

  • ADA: the currency of Cardano.
  • Anchoring: writing a short fingerprint of a record on the blockchain, so anyone can check later that it has not been changed.
  • Block explorer: a public website that shows every transaction and token on a blockchain.
  • Cardano: the public blockchain MagicLamp runs on.
  • DAO: a community that decides by vote under published rules.
  • DePIN: a network of physical devices owned by many people rather than one company.
  • DID, Phoenix DID: a digital identity whose keys belong to its owner. It can stand for a person, a company or a service. Only a person is verified biometrically and can vote.
  • Epoch: Cardano's cycle of about five days.
  • Main network (Mainnet): the live Cardano network, where tokens are real.
  • Native asset: a token on Cardano identified by its own policy, which sits in an address and can be sent.
  • Oracle: a service that brings real-world facts onto a blockchain.
  • Platform: a shared service that applications are built on.
  • Policy, policy ID: the rule set that defines a token on Cardano, and the 56-character code that identifies it.
  • Recovery phrase: the list of words a wallet shows when it is created, from which its keys can be rebuilt.
  • Safe: a container for LAMP belonging to one person or one list of recipients, under its own rules.
  • Share (pot): one of the 18 parts of the 36 billion LAMP, each set aside for one purpose.
  • Snapshot: a record of who held or delegated what at one moment.
  • Stake pool: a server, run by an operator, that produces Cardano blocks. ADA holders can delegate to it.
  • Store: a container for LAMP not yet handed out.
  • Treasury: a shared account that receives unclaimed and leftover LAMP, where it is recorded.
  • Vault: a place for LAMP that belongs to one DID. LAMP there produces MAGIC.
